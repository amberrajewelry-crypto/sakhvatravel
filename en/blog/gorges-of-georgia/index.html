Gorges & Canyons of Georgia: Truso, Dariali, Martvili, Okatse & Dashbashi

Timur · Sakhva Travel Updated 1 September 2026 12 min read
In short: Georgia is a country of gorges and canyons. The most rewarding ones are Truso with its Martian mineral springs near Kazbegi, the historic Dariali gorge on the Georgian Military Road, the Pankisi gorge in Kakheti, the turquoise Martvili canyon and the Okatse canyon with its cliff-edge walkway in western Georgia, plus Dashbashi with a glass bridge just 1.5 hours from Tbilisi. Below: what to see in each, how to get there and when to go.

Over four years as a guide I've driven to almost every gorge in Georgia — from borderland Truso to the canyons of Samegrelo. A gorge in Georgia is never just "a nice view": each one has its own character. One is about Martian springs and silence, another about history and poets, a third about turquoise water and boats, a fourth about the adrenaline of a glass bridge. This guide gathers the country's main gorges and canyons — with honest details: where you actually need a 4×4, where an ordinary car is fine, and which season to pick.

We'll start with my personal favourite — Truso — and then move across the country from Kazbegi to western Georgia.

Truso Valley — Martian springs near Kazbegi

If you asked me to name the most underrated place in Georgia, I'd say Truso. Not Kazbegi (everyone knows it). Not Kakheti (everyone goes). But a valley that 90% of tourists miss, simply because they've never heard of it. Truso lies about 10 km from Kazbegi and is usually combined with it.

Truso is where green grass suddenly ends and rust-red earth begins. Iron-rich mineral springs stain everything the colour of oxidised metal. White travertine terraces. Abandoned Ossetian villages. And a silence so dense it rings in your ears. This isn't "another pretty mountain spot" — it's another planet hidden 10 kilometres from Kazbegi.

What to see in Truso

Mineral springs. Ferrous springs paint the rock red, orange and yellow. The water bubbles up from the ground — naturally carbonated by CO₂. You can taste it: metallic but safe. Some springs are warm.

Abandoned Ossetian villages. Stone towers and houses left after past conflict. Roofs have collapsed, walls still stand. Quiet, slightly melancholic — and incredibly photogenic.

Travertine terraces. White mineral terraces like a miniature Pamukkale, plus the Zakagori fortress on a hill — the last point you're allowed to reach before the border.

How to get there, season, what to know

Access: the valley starts ~10 km from Stepantsminda; a 4×4 is essential to go deeper — the track along the Terek is rough. On foot it's about 21 km round trip along the valley floor (5–8 hours), nearly flat.

Season: May–October. In winter the road is impassable.

Border zone: Truso runs up to the Russian border. Carry your passport and stay on the trail. With an experienced driver there's no problem. Prefer to go with a guide? See our Truso valley tour.

Dariali Gorge — the gateway of the Caucasus

You pass through the Dariali gorge on the way to Kazbegi whether you planned to or not. It's a narrow canyon at the very north of Georgia, where the Georgian Military Road squeezes between sheer granite walls above the roaring Terek. The Russian border (Verkhny Lars checkpoint) is only a few kilometres away — historically this was the main gate across the Greater Caucasus, celebrated by Pushkin and Lermontov.

What to see: the monumental Dariali monastery on the banks of the Terek, cliffs hundreds of metres high, and ruined fortifications on the crags (linked to the legend of "Queen Tamar's castle"). Just 4 km away are the Gveleti waterfalls: a short 1–2 hour walk to the big waterfall, easy enough for families.

Getting there & when: the gorge is right on the highway, so an ordinary car is fine — you don't go out of your way, it's simply "on the road" to Kazbegi. Open year-round, but remember it's a border zone: keep your passport with you.

Pankisi Gorge — Kists and Sufi songs

The Pankisi gorge in Kakheti is the most unexpected place on this list. The Alazani valley near Akhmeta is home to the Kists — ethnic Vainakh people (relatives of the Chechens) who have lived here for two centuries, practise Islam and keep a unique culture. The gorge once had a heavy reputation; today it's a model of community eco- and ethno-tourism.

What to do: listen to a women's Sufi zikr — a hypnotic circular chant in the mosque of Duisi village; stay in a family guesthouse and try Kist cuisine; ride through the valley on horseback; hike up to old towers on the slopes. It's an experience about people and culture, not postcard views.

Getting there & when: ~2 hours from Tbilisi through Kakheti, ordinary car. Best season April–October. Pankisi pairs neatly with a classic Kakheti trip — vineyards plus living mountain culture in one route.

Martvili Canyon — turquoise water of Samegrelo

If Truso is about harsh high-altitude geology, Martvili is the opposite: a warm western-Georgian canyon with water of an unreal turquoise colour. The Abasha river cut through limestone to form a narrow gorge with sheer green walls, waterfalls and grottos. It was once the bathing spot of the noble Dadiani family.

What to do: the headline experience is a ride on an inflatable boat along the turquoise water between the cliffs (about 15 minutes; expect a queue in season), plus viewing platforms above the water and small waterfalls. The canyon is developed: ticket office, paths, café.

Getting there & when: Martvili is in Samegrelo, ~4 hours (280 km) from Tbilisi — best combined with Kutaisi or a trip to the sea. Ordinary car. Season April–October; the water is at its best in spring and early summer.

Okatse Canyon & Kinchkha Waterfall

Okatse is the canyon for those who like a little adrenaline. Above a chasm in Imereti, near Gordi village, a long cliff-edge metal walkway is bolted to the rock: you walk along the balcony with tens of metres of empty air and the river below. It feels like flying over the canyon — and it's completely safe.

What to do: the hanging walkway — 780 m of metal catwalk about 140 m above the river — ending at a viewing platform, plus the spectacular multi-tiered Kinchkha waterfall and a relict-forest reserve nearby. Martvili canyon and the Prometheus Cave are in the same part of Imereti — easy to combine into one full day.

Getting there & when: ~40–50 minutes from Kutaisi, ~3.5–4 hours from Tbilisi; ordinary car to the visitor centre, then on foot (often a shuttle to the walkway entrance). Season April–November; the metal walkway is slippery in rain, so bring grippy shoes.

Dashbashi Canyon — a glass bridge 1.5 hours from Tbilisi

Dashbashi is the newest and closest canyon to the capital. In the Tsalka district, a glass bridge about 240 metres long with a transparent bar-capsule in the middle was built over the canyon of the Khrami river, along with a trail to the canyon.s waterfalls and viewing platforms. This is no longer wild nature but a landscaped experience park — perfect if you only have one free day.

What to do: walk the glass bridge over the chasm, descend the trail to the canyon waterfalls, sit in the glass capsule overlooking the canyon. A good option for families and for anyone not up for a multi-hour mountain trek.

Getting there & when: ~2 hours from Tbilisi, ordinary car, open almost year-round. It's the easiest trip on this list logistically — realistically doable in half a day.

How to choose a gorge — by intent

  • Martian landscapes and silence, ready for a 4×4 → Truso (with Kazbegi).
  • Something "on the way" to Kazbegi, effortless → Dariali gorge + Gveleti waterfall.
  • Culture, people, something unlike ordinary tourism → Pankisi gorge (with Kakheti).
  • Turquoise water and a boat → Martvili canyon (western Georgia).
  • Adrenaline and a cliff-edge walkway → Okatse canyon + Kinchkha waterfall.
  • Short on time, close to Tbilisi, still spectacular → Dashbashi canyon with its glass bridge.
