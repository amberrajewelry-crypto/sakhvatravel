Georgia has become one of the top dental tourism destinations for both CIS and European patients. Treatment here costs 2–4 times less than in Western Europe, visa-free entry is open to most countries, and flights to Tbilisi are short and cheap. Below is what an implant and a crown really cost, where to look for a clinic in Tbilisi and Batumi, how to plan your visits around implantation, and how to fill the days between appointments.

Why people come to Georgia for dental work

Dental tourism means travelling to another country for dental treatment when the quality is comparable to home but the price is noticeably lower. Georgia made the shortlist for a reason: several factors came together here that many competitors lack.

First, price. Rent, wages and taxes in Georgia are lower than in the EU, so the same crown or implant costs several times less while using the same European materials and implant systems. For a patient from Germany, Israel or Scandinavia the difference in cost often covers the flight and stay — treatment in Tbilisi works out cheaper even including travel.

Second, accessibility. Citizens of most CIS countries, the EU, the UK and many others enter Georgia visa-free and can stay up to a year. No medical visa, invitations or paperwork — you just buy a ticket and fly in. Many clinics work with international and Russian-speaking patients, and some dentists trained in Russia or Europe.

Third, language and comfort. In Tbilisi you can easily get by in Russian and English, the city is familiar and safe, and around you is not a grey hospital district but a living, beautiful country where the break between visits turns into a real journey. That is what sets dental tourism to Georgia apart from a purely "teeth" trip: people come to be treated and to travel at the same time.

The name "Tbilisi" comes from the Georgian "tbili" — warm. The city grew up around warm springs as a place people came to restore their health. In a sense, medical tourism here is an ancient tradition — only now modern clinics have joined the sulfur baths.

What is treated: procedures popular with medical tourists

Most people come to Georgia for work that is expensive at home yet fits inside a trip. Here are the main areas.

  • Dental implants. The most common reason for dental tourism. Georgia offers the same global implant systems as Europe (both premium and budget lines) at a noticeably lower turnkey price. Suitable for replacing one tooth, a bridge or a full-arch rehabilitation.
  • Prosthetics and crowns. Metal-ceramic, zirconia, veneers. Many come to renew several crowns at once or redo the "smile zone" with veneers — aesthetic dentistry is well developed here.
  • Root canal treatment. Endodontics under a microscope is available in the larger Tbilisi clinics, often cheaper than at home.
  • Orthodontics. Braces and aligners are fitted here too, but it is a months-long process — for a tourist it works better as a start of treatment with follow-up at home.
  • Cleaning and whitening. Quick procedures convenient to do in a single visit alongside a check-up.

Worth a special mention are complex full-arch cases — full prosthetics on implants (All-on-4 / All-on-6 protocols). These are expensive everywhere, so this is precisely where the price gap between Georgia and Europe runs into thousands of euros. Such work requires several visits and careful planning, covered below.

Tbilisi or Batumi: where to have dental work

The two main cities for dental tourism are the capital Tbilisi and the Black Sea city of Batumi. The choice depends on how complex your case is and whether you want to combine treatment with the sea.

Tbilisi — the widest choice

Tbilisi is the country's largest medical hub. It has the most clinics, dental labs and narrow specialists, and it is easier to get a second opinion and find a dentist for a specific complex case. Convenient direct flights, a huge choice of accommodation for any budget, and the whole dental-tourism infrastructure is well established. For implants, prosthetics and anything needing a lab and several appointments, Tbilisi is the optimal option.

Batumi — treatment plus the sea

Batumi is great in summer, when you want to combine dentist visits with a beach holiday. There are fewer clinics than in the capital, but for basic procedures — fillings, crowns, simple implantation — the choice is enough. If your programme is simple and you travel in the warm season, Batumi adds the bonus of the sea between appointments.

A practical scenario many dental tourists follow: the main treatment in Tbilisi, where the choice of clinics is wider, and day trips around the country between visits. If you travel in summer and want the sea, consider Batumi — but for complex work the capital is still more reliable.

How to choose a clinic and dentist: the criteria

We deliberately do not advertise specific clinics — the choice is always yours, and it should rest on facts rather than an ad ranking. Here is what to look at so you do not get it wrong.

  • Treatment plan and quote in writing. Before any work a good clinic sends a detailed plan and a fixed quote. A vague "come over and we'll see" with no price guide is a red flag.
  • Implant brand and materials. Ask which implant system they use and what manufacturer guarantees apply. Global brands give a lifetime guarantee on the implant itself.
  • Warranty and support. Ask about the warranty on the work and what to do if a problem arises once you are home. Serious clinics provide documents and stay in touch.
  • Reviews outside advertising. Look for real patient reviews in independent sources and dental-tourism communities, not just on the clinic's own site.
  • Language. Make sure you will be understood — Russian or English, and a coordinator for international patients greatly simplifies the trip.
  • On-site diagnostics. A good clinic does a CT scan and X-ray before planning, rather than placing an implant "by eye".
Important: this article is not medical advice and not an advertisement for specific dentists. The choice of treatment method is made only by a qualified dentist after an exam and scans. Do not pick a clinic solely on the lowest price — on implants and prosthetics a headline saving can turn into a redo. Compare the plan, warranties and reputation, not just the figure in the quote.

How to plan the trip and dentist visits

The key feature of dental tourism is that treatment often cannot be done in a single day, and some work requires a break of several months. So the trip needs to be planned in advance, starting from the type of treatment.

Simple treatment — one visit

Fillings, cleaning, root canals, single crowns on ready teeth — all of this fits into one trip of 5–10 days. Day one goes on the exam and scans, then the procedures themselves with breaks. Between appointments there is plenty of free time to explore the city.

Implantation — usually two visits

Classic implantation is split into two stages. The first visit (2–5 days): diagnostics and placing the implant in the bone. Then a 3–4 month break for healing, which you spend at home. The second visit (3–5 days): fitting the crown onto the healed implant. So from start to finished smile it takes several months and two trips.

In some cases immediate-load implantation is possible — where a temporary crown goes on almost at once and the bulk is done in one trip of 7–12 days. But it is not for everyone: the dentist decides based on the bone condition and scans. Do not count on "everything in a week" in advance — diagnostics come first.

Medical tourism in Georgia beyond dentistry

Although teeth are the most common reason for medical trips, Georgia also receives patients in other areas where prices are below European levels: check-ups and diagnostics, aesthetic medicine and plastic surgery, ophthalmology, IVF and fertility programmes. The logic is the same as with dentistry: comparable quality at a markedly lower price plus convenient visa-free entry.

If you are coming to fix your teeth anyway, it makes sense to have a general check-up at the same time — run tests and basic diagnostics while you are in the country. Many clinics offer comprehensive screening programmes over one or two days. This is not medical advice but a practical observation: since the trip is about health, it is sensible to use it fully.

For any medical procedure other than dentistry, always consult a specialist doctor and verify the clinic's licences. We are a travel service and help with the trip and leisure, not with choosing a treatment method.

Pros and pitfalls of dental tourism

To make the decision balanced, here honestly are both sides.

Pros

  • A 2–4× saving on expensive work — implants, prosthetics, veneers.
  • Visa-free entry and short flights from the CIS and Europe.
  • Russian and English in communication, coordinators for international patients.
  • The chance to combine treatment with a proper holiday in a beautiful country.
  • Modern materials and global implant systems.

Pitfalls

  • Follow-up at home. If a problem arises after you return, correcting it with a local dentist is harder. Choose a clinic with a clear warranty and documents.
  • Timing. Implantation means two visits with a months-long break, not "everything in a week". Plan ahead.
  • Variable quality. As everywhere, clinics differ. A low price does not guarantee the result — compare the plan and reputation, not just the quote.
  • Travel hiccups. A delayed flight or feeling unwell can shift the treatment schedule. Build in a buffer of days.

How to book and pay for treatment

Organising the trip is easier than it seems. You can book a clinic remotely before you fly: almost all major centres communicate via WhatsApp, email or their website, accept your old scans and send a preliminary plan. This is the key step — it tells you in advance how many days to fly for and how much to budget.

On payment, Georgia is convenient: clinics accept cash lari, bank cards, and often dollars or euros. Currency exchange is favourable and ATMs are everywhere; large sums are best paid by card or bank transfer rather than carried in cash. Ask in advance whether a deposit is taken for implants and at which stage — that is normal practice when materials are ordered from abroad.

On documents: always keep the contract, receipts and warranty papers for the work done and the implants placed. If you later need a dentist's help at home, these documents show exactly what was fitted and which system. A good clinic issues them without being asked and in a language you understand.

The main bonus of medical tourism to Georgia specifically is that you will not be bored between visits. Dental treatment rarely fills the whole day: after a morning appointment you are usually free, and during the healing break between implantation stages you can even go on a proper journey. Frequently asked questions about dental treatment in Georgia

How much does dental treatment cost in Georgia?

Roughly speaking, dental treatment in Georgia is 2–4 times cheaper than in Western Europe. A filling costs about 40–90 GEL, a metal-ceramic crown 250–450 GEL, and a full implant with crown 1,500–3,000 GEL depending on the system and clinic. Exact prices depend on the clinic, implant brand and scope of work, so always request a treatment plan and quote in advance.

Is it better to have dental work in Tbilisi or Batumi?

Tbilisi offers the widest choice of clinics, labs and specialists, convenient flights and plenty of accommodation, which is why most dental tourists come here. Batumi suits those who want to combine treatment with the sea in summer. For complex cases (implants, prosthetics, orthodontics) it is easier to find a narrow specialist and a second opinion in Tbilisi.

How long does dental implantation take in Georgia?

Classic implantation means two visits: the first to place the implant (2–5 days with diagnostics), then a 3–4 month break for healing, and a second visit to fit the crown (3–5 days). With immediate-load protocols part of the work is done in one trip of 5–10 days, but this is not suitable for everyone — the dentist decides from the scans.

Do I need a visa for dental treatment in Georgia?

Citizens of many countries, including most of the CIS, the EU, the UK and the US, enter Georgia visa-free and can stay up to one year. No special medical visa is required for dental tourism — ordinary visa-free entry is enough. Check your own country's rules before travel.

What can I do between dental appointments?

Dental treatment rarely takes the whole day, so free time is perfect for getting to know the country: old Tbilisi, the sulfur baths, the wine region of Kakheti, the Kazbegi mountains. A private guide-driver adapts the tours to your appointment schedule so you can both get treated and see Georgia.
