Dezertirebi Bazaar

Tbilisi's main market is where locals shop for everything. Spices — Svan salt, khmeli-suneli, adjika — cost 3 to 4 times less here than in the tourist shops of the Old Town. Timur will introduce you to trusted vendors and explain which spices are worth taking home.

The wine section is a revelation: home wines sold straight from 5- and 10-litre plastic canisters. It sounds odd, but this is often the best wine you can buy in Tbilisi. Timur knows which sellers bring their own product — not resold bulk wine. You can fill a bottle on the spot.

Jewelry Workshops

In the lanes of the Old Town, craftsmen still make jewelry entirely by hand. The traditional Georgian technique — cloisonné enamel (minankari) — produces unique patterns featuring the pomegranate flower, a national symbol. Prices: rings ₾40–80, earrings ₾60–120, bracelets ₾80–150. Each piece is the work of a specific artisan, not a factory product.

The antique rows near the market hold Soviet-era badges, Georgian coins, old icons (replicas — originals may not be exported), and vintage household items. Timur will help you tell the genuine from the copy and negotiate a fair price.

Detailed Route with Times

10:00 — Dezertirebi Bazaar (David Agmashenebeli Avenue, 125). Tbilisi's main food market. Ground floor: seasonal fruit and vegetables (in August — figs, ₾3/kg), cheeses (sulguni from ₾8/kg, Imeretian from ₾6/kg). Upper floor: spices. Svan salt (a blend of salt, mountain herbs, and garlic) — ₾3–4 per 200 g; in Old Town souvenir shops the same product costs ₾10–15. Khmeli-suneli — ₾2–3. Adjika — fresh from ₾4, dry from ₾2.

11:00 — Home wine at Dezertirebi. In the lower section, vendors sell home wines from 5- and 10-litre containers. Red Saperavi ₾3–5/litre, white Rkatsiteli ₾3–4/litre. Timur knows which sellers bring their own wine rather than re-sell. You can take it in a plastic bottle of your choice.

11:30 — Dry Bridge Market (Kura embankment near Metechi Bridge). Open-air flea market: Soviet porcelain, Georgian jewelry, old books in Georgian and Russian. Open daily 10:00–17:00. Bargaining is standard — opening prices are typically inflated 2–3x. Timur negotiates in Georgian, which saves 30–50% compared to tourist pricing.

12:30 — Old Town Jewelry Workshops (Kote Afkhazi and Leselidze streets). Open 10:00–18:00. The cloisonné enamel technique: copper base + glass paste + kiln firing. The signature motif is the pomegranate flower. Rings ₾40–80, earrings ₾60–120, bracelets ₾80–150. This is handmade work by individual craftsmen.

13:30 — Georgian Designer Boutiques (Shardeni Street). Five to six boutiques carrying Georgian clothing, ceramics, and jewelry from local brands. Prices are higher than the market, but quality is consistent and provenance is guaranteed. Names to remember: "Tbilisi' 87", "Manavis", "Gori Dachi".

Three Insider Tips

  • At Dezertirebi, do not buy spices from the first stall near the entrance — that is the tourist zone with a 50% markup. Walk deeper into the market to the rows that serve locals.
  • Exporting antiques older than 1960 from Georgia requires a permit from the Ministry of Culture. No genuine 1940s icon, coin, or Soviet item should leave the country without documentation.
  • The best time for Dry Bridge Market is Saturday morning (10:00–12:00): the widest selection and sellers in a generous mood.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can I buy on a shopping tour in Tbilisi?

Georgian wines, chacha brandy, churchkhela, spices, adjika, ceramics, wool goods, cloisonné enamel jewelry, and souvenirs from the Old Town. The best value purchases are spices and home wine at Dezertirebi Market.

Which markets and shops will we visit?

Dry Bridge Market (antiques and unique finds), Dezertirebi Market (spices, cheeses, wines), and boutiques of Georgian designers in the Old Town around Shardeni and Leselidze streets.

Does the guide help with bargaining?

Yes, Timur knows the real prices and will help you avoid overpaying at the markets. Bargaining is expected at Dry Bridge — the guide will show you how it is done and negotiate on your behalf in Georgian, which typically saves 30–50%.

Is the Dry Bridge Market open every day?

Yes, every day from approximately 10:00 to 17:00. Saturdays have the largest selection and the most vendors. Rainy days also attract sellers — they bring covered stalls.

How much should I budget for shopping?

Many guests spend ₾50–150 on spices, wine, and small gifts. For quality enamel jewelry or antiques, budget ₾100–400. Timur will help you understand what a fair price looks like for each category.
