About the tour

The Layer Most Tours Skip

Georgia was part of the Soviet Union for 70 years. That history left its mark on the city in concrete, mosaics, and stories — and Timur knows how to read it.

This tour covers the architectural periods: constructivism of the 1920s, socialist realism of the Stalin era, Khrushchev-era housing blocks, and late Soviet modernism. But it's not just about buildings — it's about the lives lived inside them, and how Georgians navigated Soviet power while preserving their culture.

For architecture enthusiasts, history lovers, and anyone who wants to understand the modern city through its recent past.

Day schedule

Route & Schedule

Start
Rustaveli Avenue
Georgia's main boulevard, lined with Stalinist neoclassical buildings. The parliament, the opera house, the national gallery — all Soviet-era, all with stories.
Stop 1
Soviet metro station
Tbilisi's metro was built in 1966 with elaborate mosaic art. Timur takes you to the best examples and explains the iconography.
Stop 2
Constructivist Tbilisi
The 1920s avant-garde buildings in the city centre — often overlooked but architecturally extraordinary. The Stalinist period that followed deliberately obscured them.
Stop 3
Housing estates & everyday life
A walk through a typical Soviet microrayon — understanding how people actually lived, the communal vs. private tension, and what remains today.
End
The Georgian response
How Georgian culture survived Sovietization: the underground music scene, samizdat literature, religious persistence. Over coffee with Timur.
