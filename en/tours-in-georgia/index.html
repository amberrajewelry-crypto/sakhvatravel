How to choose a route for your dates

After three years and more than 500 trips on these roads, I've learned the right pick depends less on a place's rating and more on the season and who's travelling. Spring (April–May) is best for the high mountains: the snow has melted, the valleys turn green, and the passes are open. Summer heat pushes visitors toward water — the Black Sea coast, shaded canyons and cool alpine gorges. Autumn is harvest time: family cellars welcome guests during rtveli, with young wine and fresh bread on the table.

Group size matters too. Couples and solo travellers usually take a private walk through the old quarters, the sulphur baths and the cable car above the river. Families with kids and groups of friends prefer a mini-group — cheaper per person and livelier on the way. Business guests enjoy a corporate day with tastings; couples often add a sunset photo session.

Not sure what suits you? Message me on WhatsApp and I'll lay out the pros of each option for your dates and budget — free advice, and the booking commits you to nothing. Cancel up to 24 hours ahead at no cost.

What to See in Georgia in One Trip

Most visitors start in the capital: the cable car up to Narikala Fortress, the brick domes of the Abanotubani sulphur baths, the carved balconies of the old quarters. From there the road climbs into the mountains — Stepantsminda and Gergeti church beneath the 5,000-metre Mount Kazbek, medieval towers along the Georgian Military Highway, the turquoise Ananuri reservoir.

For wine, travellers head to Kakheti — family cellars of the Alazani Valley, clay qvevri, the hilltop town of Sighnaghi. The west holds the Martvili and Okatse canyons, the cave monastery of Vardzia, the scorched ridges of Davit Gareja and the spa town of Borjomi. Those with a week to spare add the high-mountain region of Svaneti with the towers of Ushguli.

How Excursion Formats Differ

A private walk means just you and the guide, with pace and stops shaped around you — chosen by couples, photographers and anyone who dislikes crowds. A mini-group of up to 7 people works out cheaper per person and is livelier on the road — popular with families and groups of friends. A master class (chacha, khachapuri, wine) is half a day of cooking and tasting. A multi-day route of 3–10 days with overnight stays suits those who want to see the whole country rather than a single region.
