The Children's Program in Detail

We start near the Metekhi Bridge — with the legend of Tbilisi's founding. Timur tells the story of King Vakhtang Gorgasali's hunt in an interactive way: children "help" the king find a wounded pheasant and a hot spring. The legend dates to the 5th century and becomes a real adventure when told this way.

The climb to Narikala Fortress comes with a mission: find the tallest tower and count the arrow slits. For older children — the story of Persian warriors and Georgian defenders. For younger ones — the tale of a dragon that lived in the fortress. Both versions keep kids fully engaged.

The cable car ride is a non-negotiable highlight. Ascending over the city gives children a bird's-eye view of Tbilisi's colorful rooftops and the Kura River winding below. At the top, the fortress ruins invite imagination — who lived here, who attacked it, where are the secret passages?

A Full Day Schedule for Children in Tbilisi

The route is designed for 3–4 hours of active time with breaks. We start at 10:00 or 16:30 — the best times to avoid heat and tourist crowds.

  • 10:00 — Rike Park on the Kura River. Playgrounds, swings, view of Narikala Fortress from below. I tell the legend: King Vakhtang Gorgasali found a hot spring in the 5th century when a wounded pheasant fell into it and healed. Children listen and immediately ask about the pheasant.
  • 10:20 — Bridge of Peace (pedestrian). Glass bridge 150 m long with an ornate canopy. Children love looking through the glass floor down at the river. I explain that the bridge was designed by an Italian architect and opened in 2010.
  • 10:35 — Cable car. Ride from Rike Park up to Narikala Fortress. Elevation gain 80 m, time in the air — 3 minutes. The gondola fits 6–8 people. Suitable from age 3; I hold hands during boarding. Price: ₾1 each way (card accepted).
  • 10:45 — Narikala Fortress (4th–17th century). Children's mission: find the tallest tower, count the arrow slits, locate where the cannons stood. For children 8+: I tell about the Mongol invasion of 1236 and how residents hid in underground passages. For younger children: the story of a dragon caught by a Georgian knight.
  • 11:30 — Walk down through Abanotubani. The sulfur bath domes — "like mushrooms," children say. I explain that the water at +37°C flows from the ground naturally, without any heating. Photo stop at the colorful domes.
  • 12:00 — Churchkhela and ice cream. In the lanes of the Old Town we pick out churchkhela — Georgian sweets made of nuts in grape juice, ₾2–3 each. Ice cream is on the guide — a firm tour tradition.
  • 12:30–13:00 — Return to the starting point or lunch at a child-friendly cafe nearby.

Adapting to Different Ages

  • Ages 4–6: Focus on fairy-tale stories, short walks, more stops. The cable car is a must — children are thrilled. Fortress visit is 15 minutes without long explanations.
  • Ages 7–10: Detail-finding missions, riddle questions, interactive elements. I explain how the fortress was built, the trade routes, what was traded in Tbilisi 500 years ago.
  • Ages 11–14: Historical facts, numbers, comparisons with the present day. How Tbilisi looked in Rustaveli's time, how the Georgian Orthodox cross differs from the Russian one.

Three Practical Tips for Parents

  • In summer, choose the 10:00 tour — by 13:00 it's +32–35°C and children tire quickly. Or take the evening option starting at 17:00 — the sunset from Narikala Hill is more beautiful than in daylight.
  • The cable car occasionally stops for maintenance — check the day before. If it's closed, we walk up the cobblestones (10 min) — the view is equally impressive.
  • Bring ₾20–30 in cash — children will definitely want to buy magnets, churchkhela, and small Georgian warrior figurines in the lanes of the Old Town.

What to Bring

  • Closed-toe shoes with grip — the cobblestones in the Old Town are uneven and loafers slip
  • Hat and water — there is no shade at the fortress, it gets very hot in summer
  • Children's sunscreen SPF 50+ — the reflection off the fortress's white walls is intense
  • Wet wipes — churchkhela and ice cream leave marks
  • A spare t-shirt for the child — ice cream is unpredictable

Frequently Asked Questions

What age is the children's tour suitable for?

From age 4. The program includes interactive stories, play-based tasks, and stops at places that interest children. The route is adjusted based on the children's ages — more fairy-tale stories and play stops for younger children, more history and legends for older ones.

Do children pay full price?

Children under 4 are free. The tour price covers the whole group (family), not per person. Contact us to clarify the price for your group size.

Is there a special children's menu at food stops?

Georgian cuisine is very child-friendly: khachapuri, khinkali, churchkhela will delight even picky eaters. Allergies and dietary preferences are taken into account in advance.

Are there rest stops for children along the route?

Yes, the route is flexible: pauses at playgrounds, ice cream along the way, and the walking pace adapts to the age and mood of the children. Short transitions, frequent stops.

How long does the cable car take?

The cable car ride itself is about 3 minutes each way. Queue time varies — up to 20 minutes in high season. We always account for this in the schedule. If the cable car is closed for maintenance, we walk up the cobblestones (10 min) — the view is the same.

Can very young children (under 3) participate?

Yes, but the route is adapted additionally. For children under 3 the program focuses on sensory experiences: the warm sulfur bath water, the colors of the Old Town, churchkhela tasting, open spaces in Rike Park. The fortress visit is shortened to 10–15 minutes.

Detailed Route

Route for children ages 4–12: cable car to Narikala (₾2.50 round trip, kids love the flight over the river), Narikala Fortress (20 min — children explore the walls and arrow slits). Then the Botanical Garden (entry ₾4, a waterfall and suspension bridge — a real adventure for school-age kids). Ice cream break at a local shop (from ₾5). Finish at Rike Park: dancing fountains (free, with light show in the evening), playground. Optional: Georgian khinkali masterclass (₾25 — they make them and then eat them). Relaxed pace, a stop every 40 minutes.
