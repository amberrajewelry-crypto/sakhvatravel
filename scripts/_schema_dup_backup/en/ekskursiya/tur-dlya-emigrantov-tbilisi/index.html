About the Tour

Tbilisi — One of the Best Cities in the World for Remote Work

In recent years Tbilisi has become a genuine hub for freelancers and digital nomads. Visa-free entry for most nationalities, a low cost of living, fast internet, and a warm atmosphere have made it one of the most popular destinations in Eastern Europe and the Caucasus. Thousands of nomads choose Tbilisi every year — and most of them spend their first week figuring out the basics while living in a tourist hotel.

This tour was created specifically to skip the "reconnaissance" phase. In 3–4 hours I'll show you the co-working spaces actually worth visiting (not just the ones Google suggests), cafés with WiFi that doesn't drop at 11am, neighborhoods for renting a flat, how to get a SIM card without overpaying, and how to open a bank account without queuing.

We start at Fabrika — a former Soviet textile factory turned into the city's most interesting creative space. Co-working on one side, container bars on the other, and an atmosphere that makes people stay in Tbilisi longer than planned. Then we walk through Vera — the neighborhood I recommend to serious nomads: shaded streets, a mix of old Tbilisi architecture and modern cafés, easy walkability, and good transport links.

Along the way I'll tell you everything I know about the practical side of nomad life here: where Georgian bureaucracy is surprisingly simple (banks, SIM cards) and where it isn't (long-stay visas, residency). Where to eat for under ₾15. Which neighborhoods are loud at night. What the nomad community looks like and how to join it.

I've lived in Tbilisi for over 10 years. I've watched this city change. I know what works for people who want to stay a month and what works for those planning a year. This tour is a conversation as much as a walk.

A Working Day in Tbilisi — What the Tour Covers

This tour is for people who've moved here for the long term, not just two weeks. The questions here are different: not "where to eat khinkali" but "where's a co-working space with decent internet", "how do I hire an accountant", "where can I find an English-speaking doctor". I live in Tbilisi and I know the answers to all of these.

10:00 — Co-working Spaces & Workspaces

We start with an overview of Tbilisi's co-working ecosystem. We visit 2–3 spaces in person: Fabrika (Agmashenebeli Avenue, central) — day pass ₾30–50, monthly ₾300–400, stable WiFi, slightly noisy. Impact Hub (Krtsanisi) — quieter, corporate atmosphere, ₾350–500/month. Terminal (Vake) — pricier (₾600+), but meeting rooms and 200+ Mbps internet. I show you what they look like inside and introduce you to residents.

11:30 — Banks & Finances: A Practical Walkthrough

We pass the branches of TBC Bank and Bank of Georgia — the two main banks for expats. TBC Start: account opened with a foreign passport in 20 minutes, card issued immediately, ₾0 monthly fee. Bank of Georgia: takes a little longer but SWIFT transfers are simpler. I cover alternatives too: Credo Bank (shorter queues), crypto exchange offices on Marjanishvili Street (exchange rate 1–2% better than the banks).

12:30 — Neighborhoods: An Overview with Prices

We drive through different neighborhoods — looking at the streets, courtyards, noise levels. Saburtalo: quiet courtyards, schools, metro, 2-bedroom ₾600–800. Vake: upmarket, green, ₾1000–1500. Gldani: cheap (₾400–500) but far from the center. Navtlugi: new residential complexes, good infrastructure, ₾700–900. Digomi: new builds near the airport, ₾500–700. My advice: don't rush into a long-term lease in the first month — better to take a short-term flat (₾2000–3000/month equivalent) and choose your neighborhood at your own pace.

14:00 — Healthcare, Food, Everyday Life

I show you a trusted clinic with English-speaking doctors (Mediindustry, Vake — appointments from ₾40), Synevo laboratory (tests 2–3 times cheaper than in Western Europe), pharmacies. I explain Georgian healthcare: basic insurance exists for citizens, for expats it's commercial (from $30/month). We finish at Dezerter Bazaar — I show you where to buy a week's groceries on a ₾100–120 budget per person.

Practical Tips

  • Before the tour: send me your list of questions on WhatsApp the day before we meet. I'll prepare specific answers, not generic ones.
  • Sole trader in Georgia: an individual entrepreneur (IE) can be registered online in 1–2 days. Tax rate — 1% of turnover up to 500,000 GEL/year (Small Business status). I'll explain the details on the tour.
  • Language: in the center and in business, everyone speaks Russian or English. Outside the center — Georgian and English. The first 3 words in Georgian open every door.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this tour differ from a regular sightseeing excursion?

The focus is entirely practical: neighborhoods for living, rentals, banks, SIM cards, co-working spaces, schools, healthcare. A regular excursion shows you landmarks — this one helps you actually settle in and orient yourself for a new life in the city.

Do I need to already be living in Tbilisi to take this tour?

No. The tour is also ideal for people who are just planning a move — you'll see the city through the eyes of a local and can decide whether Tbilisi suits your lifestyle before you commit.

How long does the tour last?

4–5 hours, on foot and by car through different neighborhoods: Vake, Saburtalo, Didube, Marjanishvili, Sololaki. The exact route can be adjusted to your priorities.

How fast is the internet in Tbilisi?

Very fast by global standards. Most apartments have fiber at 100–1000 Mbps. Co-working spaces typically provide 100+ Mbps symmetric connections. Mobile 4G/LTE is reliable throughout the city. During the tour I personally verify speeds at every café and co-working stop.

Do I need a visa to live in Georgia?

Most nationalities (EU, US, UK, Australia, and many others) can stay visa-free for 365 days or more. I always provide current, nationality-specific information on the tour — not outdated generic advice.
