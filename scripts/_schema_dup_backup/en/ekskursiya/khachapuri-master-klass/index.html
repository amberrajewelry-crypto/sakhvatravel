About the Class

The class takes place in a private kitchen in the Old Town, run by a local Georgian family. Tamara — a third-generation cook — will teach you to make the dough, work the cheese, and shape each type of khachapuri by hand.

We start with Imeruli — the most classic. Kefir-based dough (or yeasted water dough), filled with imeruli cheese, a fresh brine cheese that is saltier than suluguni. Then Megruli: the same round, but an additional layer of grated suluguni melted on top. We finish with Adjarian: a boat-shaped open bread with an egg and butter.

A Brief History of Khachapuri

The word "khachapuri" combines "khacho" (curd cheese) and "puri" (bread). The earliest recipes date to the 12th century. In 2019, khachapuri was added to Georgia's list of traditional cultural heritage. Today the dish has its own informal economic index: the "Khachapuri Index" tracks ingredient prices in Georgia's major cities as a measure of local inflation.

At the end of the class everything you have baked goes straight onto the table. A glass of Georgian wine (Tsinandali or Rkatsiteli) is included. Tamara hands out recipes with exact gram weights. See also: Gastro Tour of Tbilisi.

Step-by-Step: What Happens During the Class

10:00 — Meet at the bakery on Kote Meskhi Street in the Old Town. Introduction to Tamara, a short talk on khachapuri history and the differences between regions. Tea with churchkhela while the oven heats up.

10:20 — Imeruli Khachapuri. Mix the dough: premium flour + kefir + yeast. Key point: do not overwork it — the dough must be soft, like a soft earlobe. Filling: imeruli cheese (brine-cured, saltier than suluguni). Shape the round flat, seal the cheese inside without any gaps. Bake 15–18 minutes at 220°C.

10:50 — Megruli Khachapuri. Same base as Imeruli, but with an extra layer of grated suluguni spread across the top — hence the name "Megrelian double." Stronger cheese flavour, golden bubbling crust. Bake 12–15 minutes.

11:20 — Adjarian Khachapuri. The most complex and the most photogenic. Shape the dough into a "boat" — an open form. Add the cheese filling (mixed imeruli + suluguni). Crack a raw egg into the centre. Add a 25g knob of butter. Bake 8–10 minutes. Important: the egg should remain semi-runny — do not overcook it. You eat it by tearing off the bread sides and dragging them through the yolk.

12:00 — Tasting. Everything fresh from the oven goes on the table. A glass of Georgian wine. Tamara shares the written recipes with exact weights. The conversation turns to where in Tbilisi locals actually eat the best khachapuri — never in tourist-facing restaurants.

Three Practical Tips

  • Come hungry — three types of khachapuri is a full meal, not a snack.
  • The class runs in a private home kitchen, not a restaurant — the atmosphere is informal, ask any question you like about Georgian cooking.
  • The Adjarian khachapuri is technically straightforward, but the ratio of dough to cheese matters: 60% dough, 40% filling. Less dough and the boat collapses.
