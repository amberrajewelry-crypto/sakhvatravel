# Quora: Is it worth hiring a private guide in Tbilisi?

---

**Answer from: Licensed Guide, Tbilisi**

Short answer: for most visitors staying 2–4 days, yes — but only if you hire the right person. A bad guide is worse than no guide.

Let me give you the actual pros, cons, and price reality:

---

## What you actually get with a private guide

**The main value isn't information — it's context.** You can look up the history of Narikala fortress or Metekhi Church before you go. What a good guide does is make those facts meaningful *while* you're standing in front of them: why this particular spot, why the Persians built this way, what changed under Russian rule, what the Soviet period left behind that you're still walking past today. Context in real time, adjusted to what you find interesting.

**Access to places you'd miss or avoid.** A guide knows that the courtyard behind that restaurant on Shardeni Street has a 19th-century fountain that nobody photographs. That the women selling churchkhela near Anchiskhati are from the same family that's been doing it for four generations. That the sulfur bath in Abanotubani that doesn't look like much from outside has the best hot spring in the neighborhood.

**Language buffer.** In the Old Town, most interaction is manageable without Georgian or even Russian. Go five streets off the tourist track and this changes. Having someone who speaks the language — and importantly, knows the city — removes friction.

---

## The honest downside

You're locked into someone else's pace and priorities to some degree. Even with a flexible private guide, you're not as free as wandering alone with a map. If you're the type of traveler who wants to sit somewhere for two hours without agenda, a guided tour isn't for you.

Also: the guide market in Tbilisi has a significant number of low-quality operators. Someone approaching you near Rustaveli offering a tour is almost certainly unlicensed and supplementing with restaurant commissions.

## How to verify a guide before booking

- Ask for their full name and look them up on the Georgian National Tourism Administration licensed guide registry
- Request specific references or reviews from previous clients, not just generic star ratings
- Ask a test question: what's the difference architecturally between Sioni Cathedral and Metekhi Church? A licensed professional answers without hesitation.
- Confirm the language level upfront — "I speak English" and "I guide fluently in English" are different things

For Russian-speaking visitors specifically: finding a guide who is genuinely fluent in Russian (not just conversational) matters for the quality of historical and cultural explanation. There are good options — [Sakhva Travel's guide service](https://sakhva-travel.com/en/gid-v-tbilisi-na-russkom/) focuses on exactly this market.

---

## My recommendation

If you're in Tbilisi for 3+ days: book one private half-day tour on day one or two for orientation and cultural context. Then explore independently for the rest of your trip with that foundation in place. You'll see more, understand more, and make better decisions about how to spend your remaining time.

It's not about being shown where to go — it's about having the framework to understand what you're looking at once you get there.
