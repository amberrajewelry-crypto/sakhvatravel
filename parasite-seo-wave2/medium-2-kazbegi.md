# Kazbegi Day Trip from Tbilisi: Everything They Don't Tell You

*By Giorgi Sakhvadze, tour guide — Tbilisi, Georgia*

---

Kazbegi is on every Georgia itinerary. The photo of Gergeti Trinity Church sitting on a rocky spur with Mount Kazbek looming behind it has probably been downloaded a million times. What travel blogs don't usually tell you: the road to get there is one of the most dramatic drives in the Caucasus, the weather can ruin the whole thing in minutes, and the decisions you make in the first hour of your trip will determine whether you come back with the photo — or just a story about clouds.

I've done this route more times than I can count. Here's what actually matters.

## Timing Is Everything

The Georgian Military Highway from Tbilisi to Kazbegi runs through the Greater Caucasus range. At Jvari Pass (elevation 2,379 meters), the weather operates on its own logic, completely disconnected from what Tbilisi was doing when you left.

**Leave before 8:00 AM.** The mountain is most often clear in the morning. By early afternoon, clouds move in and can completely obscure Mount Kazbek and the church. Tourists who leave at 10 AM thinking they have a full day often arrive to find nothing visible above 1,800 meters.

**Check multiple forecasts the night before.** Weather apps are optimistic about mountain conditions. I use a combination of Windy.com (set it to check cloud cover at 2,500–3,000m elevation) and local Telegram weather channels. If the forecast shows cloud cover above 70% in the afternoon, consider rescheduling.

**Don't book a non-refundable tour for your first available day.** Give yourself two days of flexibility if you can. Nothing is worse than spending $80 on a guide and car only to arrive at a wall of gray fog.

## The Drive Itself

The Military Highway is 157 kilometers. In normal conditions, it takes about 2.5–3 hours one way from Tbilisi. But the road is the experience, not just the means of getting there.

**Ananuri Fortress** (about 70km in) sits at the edge of the Zhinvali reservoir — turquoise water, medieval towers, a small church with frescoes. Most tours stop here for 20–30 minutes. Don't skip it even if you're in a hurry.

**Gudauri** is a ski resort town at 2,196 meters where the road passes through some of the most exposed mountain terrain. In winter, the pass occasionally closes due to snow or avalanche risk. Check the Georgian Road Department site (roads.ge) the morning of your trip if you're going between November and April.

**The Friendship Monument** at the top of Jvari Pass is a Soviet-era curved panoramic overlook. The view is spectacular — if it's clear. Often it's inside a cloud. Stop anyway if visibility is good; it takes five minutes and the valley panoramas are unlike anything else on the route.

## Gergeti Church: Hike vs. Drive

This is the question everyone asks. The church sits at 2,170 meters above the village of Stepantsminda (which most people still call Kazbegi, the old Soviet name). Getting there involves either a 3–4 hour round-trip hike gaining 650 meters of elevation, or a 4x4 vehicle that can drive the dirt track up the hill.

**The hike**: More rewarding. The trail starts near the village, passes through forest and then open alpine meadow, and arrives at the church with the full dramatic reveal. Allow 1.5–2 hours up, 1–1.5 hours down depending on your fitness. The path is not technical — no climbing required — but the altitude makes some people breathless. Bring water and dress in layers.

**The drive**: Takes 20 minutes each way but requires a local 4x4 (usually a UAZ minivan or similar). It's the right choice if you're short on time, have mobility issues, or if the weather is closing in fast and you want to reach the summit before visibility drops. Expect to pay 30–50 GEL to a local driver who knows the track.

**My recommendation**: If you're reasonably fit and the weather is clear when you arrive in the village — hike. The view as you crest the final rise and the church appears against the mountain is one of those moments that justifies the whole trip. If weather is uncertain, take the car up first to guarantee you see it, then walk down.

## Costs in 2025/2026

- **Shared minibus (marshrutka)**: 10–15 GEL each way from Didube station in Tbilisi. No guarantees on departure time, no air conditioning, no flexibility.
- **Private car rental with driver from Tbilisi**: 200–300 GEL for the full day (approximately $70–110). Split between 2–4 people, this is the most practical option for most travelers.
- **Organized group tour**: 60–90 GEL per person. Usually includes transport, a guide, and Ananuri stop. The guide quality varies enormously.
- **Private guided tour**: If you want a guide who speaks your language fluently, knows the history of the Georgian Military Highway in depth, and has contacts in Stepantsminda for local experiences, budget 350–500 GEL per person for a private day tour. ## What to Do Beyond the Church

Most tourists arrive, photograph the church, eat khinkali in the village, and leave. That's fine. But if you want more:

**Gveleti waterfalls**: A 2-hour walk from the village along the Terek river gorge. Not crowded, very beautiful.

**Betlemi Cave Monastery**: A more challenging hike above Gergeti — requires chains and some exposure. Only for confident hikers.

**The village itself**: Stepantsminda has improved significantly in the last decade. There are decent guesthouses, restaurants serving Mtiulian (highland Georgian) cuisine, and a local market worth browsing.

**Stay overnight**: One night in Stepantsminda changes the experience entirely. The morning light on Kazbek before 8 AM, with the village quiet, is something you can't get on a day trip.

## The Reality Check

Kazbegi has become crowded. On summer weekends, the road up to the church can back up with 4x4s and the church courtyard fills with tour groups. This is still a thousand times less congested than, say, the Amalfi Coast — but it's no longer a hidden gem.

Go anyway. Go early. Check the weather. Don't panic if the mountain is in cloud when you arrive — Caucasus weather moves fast, and a clear window often opens in the first hour. Bring a proper jacket regardless of what Tbilisi weather suggested when you left.

The mountain is real. The church is extraordinary. On a clear morning, with Kazbek fully visible above you, the trip justifies itself completely.
